# Jeff Electric interactive systems case

Approved brief: the user's new interactive implementation cycle, based on Sites source 4d09782373820e9f429bbec1a5a166142294f75a. Design decisions are delegated; no interview or routine confirmation is needed.

- Audience/task: a recruiter or business owner wants to understand how Josh frames a problem and makes a practical design decision.
- Subject: Jeff Electric alone. The map is a proposed information flow, not observed operation.
- Brand: existing Manrope, white/blue, navy and cobalt. Preserve the real portrait and public website screenshot.
- Material: restrained translucent white surfaces with opaque fallback. No generated imagery or new asset service.
- Structure: six normal-flow chapters with native deep links, a named chapter index and selectable workflow components.
- Motion: unchanged upstream flow engine, short entry translations and a connector progress trace. No scroll lock, scrubbed video, pinning, parallax photos or cursor capture.
- Interaction: native radio groups, pressed-state node buttons with wrapping arrow navigation, full text equivalents and a reset that confirms the restored state.
- Delivery: exact-source candidate for the existing owner-private Site. Browser QA and publication require the supported Sites runtime.

## Feeling curve and peak

Discover: recognition, a concrete contact-to-action gap.
Map: clarity, reported state and proposed ownership are separated.
Design: control, selecting a component exposes its inputs, review and evidence gap.
Build: understanding, one requirement becomes a testable prototype loop.
Choose: confidence, practical costs and maintenance responsibilities are explicit.
Adopt: readiness, people rehearse a handoff and retain a manual path.

The peak is selecting Human decision: the visitor sees exactly why a draft cannot approve itself. The ending asks about the visitor's process and links to Josh's contact.

## Grammar and device decisions

Chosen grammar: an interactive field guide, close to the chaptered editorial grammar. Preserve the existing header because native navigation is a verified product constraint. The other collection grammars (cinematic worlds, product reveal, portfolio gallery, boutique store, event countdown, fixed editorial stage and dashboard) would distract from the inspectable reasoning or require unsupported imagery and live data.

Selected devices: normal flow acts, entry reveal, named chapter progress and code-native connector highlights. Deliberately omit the kit's four-device marketing exercise: user constraints favor a readable, small dependency surface. The engine does not hide essential text; scripts failing leaves the guide readable. On narrow or short viewports the chapter nav is not sticky. Reduced motion keeps the complete composition and does not mount the engine.
